### 2.2 What the numbers say

1. **Upstream's plain SLRU already reaches the M1 hit-rate target**: 84.4% at 6.5 GB, 90.8% at 9.4 GB (Strata: 85.4%
   from 9.16 GiB). Caveat: no routing profile and no held-out split exist yet; the cache warms across the 10 decode
   prompts in sequence, which is how a real session behaves too.
2. **And decode is about 30% slower than stock** (7-8 vs 10.6-10.9 tok/s). Hit rate is not the bottleneck.
3. **The time is in fetching misses, not in the GPU drains.** Time inside `ggml_backend_synchronize` is about 0.8 ms
   per token (48 layers): Fable's lever 1 (remove the drain) is worth little here. The **71-88 ms per token after
   the sync** is the host planning fills and issuing uploads from the mmap: 69 MiB in about 73 ms at 9.4 GB, so about
   0.95 GB/s effective. The copy probe (2.3) shows why: misses mostly fault to the NVMe (0.41 GB/s at queue depth 1),
   with free RAM at 0.06-0.18 GiB throughout. Stock is faster because the CPU computes its host-side experts in
   place instead of copying them over PCIe.
4. **Prefill is 2-3x slower with every expert layer host-resident** (pp4k 34-54 vs 112; pp16K 50-108 vs 150): every
   ubatch uploads the used experts of all 48 layers, only partly from the cache.
5. **Every arm collapses after a 16K prompt** (3.2-5.1 tok/s): the prefill flushes the page cache that decode needs.
6. **The noise floor is tighter than feared**: decode r1 vs r2 within about +-5% for every arm (stock 10.6/10.9,
   9.4 GB 7.9/7.3 with r1 contaminated); prefill within about +-8%. Decisions in this campaign need differences above
   about 10%.
7. **Floor (8 GB cap, partial):** Swift with a 2.4 GB cache: 5.6 tok/s median decode, 63.7% hit, 243 MiB uploaded per
   token, 118 ms host per token. **Below the 12 tok/s floor** on the one run, and the ballast left only **6,890 MiB**
   free (the display already holds about 1.3 GB), so the simulation was harsher than an 8 GB card with no display.

### 2.4 Exactness (greedy text and top-3 probabilities on the 10 decode prompts)

| Pair | Identical |
|---|---|
| any arm with itself across rounds (r1 vs r2) | 10/10 every arm: deterministic |
| cache 6,500 vs 9,400 | **10/10**: placement-independent, as the design predicts |
| cache 4,000 vs 6,500 or 9,400 | **7/10**: not exact |
| stock vs any cache arm | 2/10 (expected: CPU and GPU round differently) |

**Why 4,000 breaks it** (checked in the trace): at 4 GB the three IQ1_M layers (8, 13, 37, a 3-layer group with a
small byte share) had 26 multi-token prompt steps through the cache against 29 for every other layer and at 6.5 GB.
The 3 missing steps left no `S` or `L` line, so the cache's capacity check (`n_tokens x n_expert_used` above the
group's slots, `llama-moe-cache.cpp:503`) sent them off the cache path, and a small batch of host-resident experts is
computed on the **CPU**. 3 CPU-computed prompt steps = the 3 prompts whose text changed. This is the design's
capacity invariant (C-24) and its GPU-only parity rule (C-10) happening for real: **exact mode needs every group to
have enough slots for the largest ubatch on the slot-map path, or a forced GPU path for the overflow.**

## 3. What this changes in the design

- **M1 is reshaped around the miss path, not the residency policy.** The ranked policy (P2) is worth a few points of
  hit rate at most where SLRU already gives 84-91%; the gain is in serving misses from RAM instead of the NVMe.
- **Minimal P3 moves into M1** (as v2 already allowed if the endurance check failed; it did): a pinned staging ring,
  a ranked bounded RAM tier fed by unbuffered reads, so misses upload at 6.6-10 GB/s instead of 0.4-1 GB/s, and a
  prefill that cannot flush the hot set.
- **Lever ranking update:** drain removal (was #1) drops; miss sourcing (was #4) is #1; the CPU lane (class A) looks
  stronger than estimated on this machine, because the CPU computing in place beat PCIe uploads here (stock 10.7 vs
  7.6 tok/s); it stays opt-in.
- **Budget split per group must respect the capacity invariant** (the IQ1_M finding): P3 of the design (budgets by
  need) gets a hard floor per group = slots for one ubatch on the slot-map path.
- **The floor needs its own model**: Flash-Next IQ2_XS at 8 GB is at 5.6 tok/s on the one partial run; the floor
  candidates (Coder, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B class, Gemma) are still to be measured.
